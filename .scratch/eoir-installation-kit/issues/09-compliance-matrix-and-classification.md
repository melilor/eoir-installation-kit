## Answer

Closed on 2026-09-18.

**The classification is argued, not asserted.** `model/compliance.yaml` records the
classification as `OPEN` with the outcome `MAJOR` proposed, on nine appreciable-effect
criteria: weight and balance, structural strength, electrical system, avionics and data
interfaces, environmental protection, operating limitations and flight manual, instructions
for continued airworthiness (all `YES`), flight characteristics and safety assessment (both
`OPEN`, with the reason). The approval route is 21.A.97 unless the design organisation
concludes a minor change and approves it under 21.A.95; the privileges relied on are
21.A.263(c)(1) classification, (c)(2) minor changes and (c)(5)/(8) major change under
privilege. Three open items are recorded, including the platform procedure.

**The register assigns every requirement to exactly one document.** Thirteen documents:
two drawings (`DWG-001`, `DWG-002`), six analyses (`AN-001`–`AN-006`), two test documents
(`TP-001`, `TP-002`), the ICA (`ICA-001`), the generated compliance checklist (`CS-001`) and
the flight manual supplement (`FMS-001`). The `COMPLIANCE-COVERAGE` rule fails the build when
a requirement has no document or two, and `COMPLIANCE-FILE` fails when a document declares a
file that does not exist. The matrix is generated by `tools/compliance.py` into
`docs/compliance-matrix.md` (requirement, source, verification cases, method, document,
document state, evidence state) and CI checks that it is current.

**The ICA is outlined, not invented.** `docs/ica.md` follows the structure of 14 CFR 27.1529
and Appendix A — general, limitations, servicing, scheduled inspections, wear items,
structural repair, troubleshooting, parts list — with the inspection table and the open items
(intervals, torque values, airworthiness limitations) written down instead of filled with
plausible numbers.
